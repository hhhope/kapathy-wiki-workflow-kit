## Source-Class Notes

- `primary` means company page, engineering blog, official research report, or
  direct primary artifact. It can support report claims after normal reading.
- `secondary` means media coverage, podcast mirror, WeChat article, or case
  writeup by a tool vendor. It can route claims but should not be the final
  source for hard facts.
- `candidate` means landscape completeness. It is useful for search breadth but
  should not shape conclusions until read.

## Broad Patterns From The Expanded Scan

1. Agent work is moving from chat/session to durable work objects: ticket, PR,
   spec, repo rule, CI job, workflow queue, finance policy, or support case.
2. Stronger examples make the company legible to agents before expecting agent
   output: repo rules, system of record, tests, evals, logs, permissions,
   review packets, and reusable skills.
3. People model is the real question. The scarce person is the end-to-end owner
   who can set goals, decide tradeoffs, and inspect results, not the person who
   only operates a single tool.
4. Product and design move closer to execution because agents can turn well
   specified intent into reviewable artifacts. This raises, not lowers, the bar
   for taste and acceptance criteria.
5. Payment and finance examples raise the governance bar: "mostly correct" is a
   failure when money, compliance, and customer trust are involved.
6. CEO memo cases show AI is entering headcount, hiring, performance, and
   contractor strategy. These are organization-design signals, not mere tooling
   stories.
7. Research and incident sources push back against vendor optimism: AI can add
   review load, stability risk, trust gaps, security exposure, toolchain sprawl,
   and false productivity perception.
