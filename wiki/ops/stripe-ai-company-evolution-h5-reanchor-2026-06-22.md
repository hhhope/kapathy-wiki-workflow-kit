## Trigger

The user rejected the current H5 framing because it still read like abstract
commentary instead of a deep research output:

- "支付 API 起点" and "第一性原理" sounded like unsupported framing.
- "AI 到底改变了哪些金融对象" sounded like an inconsistent framework label.
- The page did not clearly answer what the 1000 sources were doing.

- A later correction rejected the market/acquisition block as isolated and asked
  for market position, acquisitions, company operations, technical events, AI

  system.
- A later correction rejected the market-share wording as too dry and too

- The user then invoked `$superpowers:brainstorming` and rejected the current

## Re-anchored North Star

Implementation is stopped. The H5 must be redesigned before further edits.

The H5 should behave like a research argument, not a pile of source categories:

1. Start from one explicit reader question and one explicit thesis.
2. Convert source material into a small number of analytical views that have
   different jobs.
3. Use timeline, market position, acquisition, technology, and AI transaction
   evidence to support that thesis, not as parallel modules.
4. Show growth and relative position through understandable visuals, with
   calculation formulas hidden or secondary unless the reader needs them.
5. Keep boundaries visible, but do not let disclaimers become the content.

## Rejected Framing

- Do not lead with broad phrases such as "first principles" unless the evidence
  directly supports them.
- Do not use "AI changes financial objects" as a visible reader-facing chapter
  title.
- Do not present word clouds as research depth.
- Do not imply that 1000 sources have all been read when the actual state is a
  queue with promoted evidence.

## 2026-06-22 Seventh Drift Correction

The user clarified that the analysis still missed a more basic financial split:
one direction is how transaction relationships are created and fulfilled;
another direction is how credit relationships are granted and recovered.

Corrected requirement:

- The H5 must stop treating payment as a single action. It must separate two
  financial-object lines:
  - transaction relationship line: how a transaction relationship is created,
    confirmed, authorized, fulfilled, settled, disputed, and attributed;
  - credit relationship line: how a credit relationship is granted, used,
    priced, monitored, repaid, and recovered.
- Stripe Capital, BNPL payment methods, Financial Connections, Identity, Radar,
  Capital underwriting, ML platform, Issuing, and spending controls must be
  organized as the credit relationship line, not as isolated product mentions.
- The credit line must explain the mechanism: who grants credit, what data
  supports underwriting, how limits and pricing are set, where repayment comes
  from, and how delinquency, fraud, disputes, and regulation are handled.
- Stripe Capital is especially important because public Stripe material ties
  eligibility to Stripe payment volume/history and repayment to a fixed
  percentage of daily sales. That supports the thesis that transaction data can
  become credit underwriting and repayment infrastructure.
- BNPL must be treated as checkout-embedded credit/deferred payment. Provider
  details such as affordability checks, APR, installment terms, and regulatory
  status need separate primary-source follow-up before public-ready claims.
- AI-era payment analysis must ask: when an Agent acts, is it spending a user's
  existing money, using a pre-authorized credential, drawing on a platform
  credit line, triggering BNPL, or using merchant working capital? Without this,
  the H5 only explains payment execution and misses the credit/risk half.

Implementation remains stopped. The next H5 revision must make the transaction
relationship line and credit relationship line visible in the structure, timeline,
visual design, and acceptance criteria.

## 2026-06-22 Eleventh Drift Correction

The user accepted parts of Section 6.0 but rejected the conclusion direction:
the draft still implied AI itself creates the need for infrastructure. The user
clarified that embedded payments, embedded finance, object models, billing,
risk, compliance, and settlement should evolve even without AI.

Corrected thesis:

- AI changes the cost curve; it is not the subject of financial survival. It
  lowers coding, integration, testing, and customization costs, which makes
  design quality more important.
- Avoid the phrase "AI is downgraded." The clearer framing is: AI is not the
  subject of survival. The subject is human, organizational, merchant,
  platform, regulatory, network, and user relationships; AI changes the cost
  curve for implementation and coordination.
- The durable problem is design under complexity: transaction relationships and
  credit relationships need to be decomposed into objects, APIs, billing rules,
  authorization boundaries, risk controls, settlement paths, dispute handling,
  and compliance boundaries.
- Design is entropy reduction. The strategic value is not "adding AI"; it is
  compressing merchants, platforms, consumers, banks, card networks, stablecoin
  rails, regulation, and exception cases into a usable, auditable system.
- AI Hub / traffic-forwarding platforms and payment companies follow different
  strategies. AI Hub is closer to wholesale routing: aggregate traffic, route
  model usage, meter consumption, and earn spread or bargaining power. Payment
  companies need an ecosystem-combination strategy: AI plus object models,
  compliance, risk, billing, settlement, and partner networks.
- In payment scenarios, customers can use AI to assemble their own flows, but a
  payment company that fuses AI into payments, billing, credit, risk, and
  compliance can offer smoother and more reliable outcomes. This is the
  "combination punch" and ecosystem integration argument.
- Billing, custom payment terms, usage billing, merchant credit, receivables,
  repayment, tax, risk, and dispute workflows are the practical imagination
  space. The public conclusion should not stop at agent payments or wallets.
- Financial innovation is not unconstrained breakthrough. The stronger claim is
  innovation within regulation and toward new consensus: authorization,
  liability, consumer/merchant protection, risk control, settlement, and dispute
  rules must become acceptable to networks, regulators, platforms, merchants,
  and users.
- Do not claim the whole web has been exhaustively searched. Use representative
  external anchors instead: NIST AI RMF for risk/design context, OECD AI
  Principles for human-centred accountability, and AP2/payment protocols for
  authorization and accountability in agent payments.

Implementation remains stopped. The next design and H5 draft must make this
thesis visible in the conclusion, not only in Section 6.
